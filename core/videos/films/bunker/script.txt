It's eleven fifty two on a Friday night. You're on the last train home, half asleep against the window.

Then every phone in the carriage lights up at once.

Emergency alert. Take shelter now. This is not a drill.

Nobody moves. There's a name for that. Normalcy bias. Your brain keeps insisting everything is fine.

You break the spell first. The doors slide open, and you run.

Down is good. The more concrete and earth above you, the better your odds.

Then the tunnel behind you turns white. Don't look back. A flash like that can burn your eyes from miles away.

Light arrives first. The blast wave follows at about the speed of sound. Roughly one kilometre every three seconds.

You count. Nine. Eight. You dive behind a pillar and cover your head.

The whole station shakes. Dust pours from the ceiling. The lights die, then flicker back in a dull red.

You're alive. And surviving the blast was the easy part.
