## Formula
- **Voice**: second person, present tense ("You are asleep. It's ... Your alarm is set for ..."). Calm male narrator, short sentences, deliberate pauses, dry humour every minute or two.
- **Teaching inside the story**: every 30-60 s a real survival fact lands as a rule ("normalcy bias", flash blindness, the 7/10 fallout rule, litres of water per day, CO2 build-up), often with simple arithmetic. Facts are what make it re-watchable and shareable.
- **Structure of an episode**: ordinary moment -> alarm -> decision under a clock (countdowns: "9 seconds ... 5 ... 3") -> survival -> inventory with numbers -> day-by-day montage ("Day one ... Day four ... Day 14") -> a cliffhanger sound or voice at the very end.
- **Picture**: one consistent character (round white head, brown hair, stick body, a cap that becomes his symbol), fully painted, detailed comic backgrounds (bedroom, street chaos with onomatopoeia, field cross-section of the shelter), thick dark outlines, warm flat colour with soft shading. Small graphic signs carry emotion: sweat drop, "?!", thought bubbles, sound-wave arcs, a glowing phone.
- **Motion: none.** Measured: after a slight zoom in the first 5.5 s, consecutive frames are identical (difference 0.0) until a hard cut; a new still every ~3-4 s. The quality is entirely in the illustration and the writing.
- **Thumbnails**: the character close-up with one strong expression, the round hatch, a mushroom cloud or snow; "Part N" handwritten top-right; no headline text.
- **Sound** (*inferred*, not heard): narration, ambient drone, effects on the big moments.

## Our version (what we add)
The same writing pattern and painted-comic look, but **animated**: a rigged character that walks, runs, flinches, blinks and emotes; moving lights, parallax, camera moves, shake on impact, every effect timed to the narration. Backgrounds are drawn in code (vector comic style with a hand-drawn wobble) unless the user approves credits for AI-painted plates.

## Transfer rules
- Copy: second-person present tense, fact-per-minute rhythm, countdown beats, cliffhanger ending, one recurring character with one symbol object, compilation strategy.
- Change: the premise details (time, place, family backstory, the shelter), every sentence, the character design (our own symbol, not a blue cap), the art.
- Never: their script lines, their character, their thumbnails or frames. Facts only from a source in the notes.
